\section{Protein nanoparticle vaccines}
\label{sec:pnp-vaccines}

The clinical success of mRNA vaccines during the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic established
nucleic-acid delivery as a fast, programmable vaccine modality: an antigen can be
encoded, manufactured and deployed within weeks of a genomic sequence becoming
available.

Nevertheless, a bare secreted antigen is often weakly immunogenic, and mRNA
formulations face practical constraints around cold-chain storage, transient and
variable expression, and reactogenicity.

A complementary strategy is to present the antigen not as a soluble monomer but
arrayed on the surface of a self-assembling \emph{protein nanoparticle}. By
displaying many copies of an antigen in a dense, repetitive, virus-like geometry,
these immunogens crosslink B-cell receptors and engage the immune system far more
potently than the corresponding soluble antigen. Designed nanoparticle vaccines
have elicited markedly stronger and more durable neutralising-antibody responses
across several pathogens, including SARS-CoV-2
\parencite{walls_elicitation_2020} and respiratory syncytial virus
\parencite{marcandalliInductionPotentNeutralizing2019a}, and multivalent designed
immunogens have conferred protection in animal models
\parencite{hunt_multivalent_2022}. Natural and engineered self-assembling
scaffolds such as lumazine synthase provide versatile platforms for multivalent,
cross-protective antigen presentation
\parencite{ladenstein_second_2020,joseph_lumazine_2025}, and the broader
engineering of self-assembling protein nanoparticles for therapeutic delivery is
now an active field \parencite{olshefskyEngineeringSelfAssemblingProtein2022,
divine_designed_2021}.\\

Crucially, because the scaffold is itself a protein, the nanoparticle and its
displayed antigen can be genetically encoded and delivered as a single mRNA
construct, so that the immunogen self-assembles \emph{in vivo}. This
``mRNA-launched'' route combines the manufacturing speed of nucleic-acid vaccines
with the superior immunogenicity of particulate display, and computationally
designed mRNA-launched nanoparticle immunogens have been shown to elicit
protective antibody and T-cell responses in mice
\parencite{hendricks_computationally_2025}. The vaccine platform we target in this
work is therefore \emph{fully protein based}: a design goal that, as we argue in
Chapter~\ref{ch:comp-design}, is unusually well matched to the capabilities of
modern generative protein models.

The appeal of symmetric protein assemblies as scaffolds is not accidental. Nature
routinely builds functional machinery from symmetric, self-assembling protein
subunits, from icosahedral viral capsids
\parencite{twarock_structural_2019} to bacterial microcompartments and
protein-based organelles \parencite{yeates_protein-based_2008} and even engineered
assemblies that encapsulate their own genome
\parencite{butterfield_evolution_2017}. Symmetry lets a large, robust container be
built from a small number of distinct parts, which is exactly the property that
makes these architectures attractive both to evolution and to the protein
designer.

\section{Protein design}
\label{sec:protein-design}

Designing the scaffolds described above is a protein-design problem: we must
specify an amino-acid sequence that folds into, and assembles into, a prescribed
three-dimensional architecture. This section sketches how the field has moved from
physics-based methods to today's machine-learning approaches.

\subsection{Physics-based design}
\label{subsec:physics-design}

The first generation of computational protein design was grounded in biophysics:
candidate sequences were threaded onto a target backbone and scored with an
energy function approximating the physics of folding, with the conformational and
sequence search handled by algorithms such as those in the Rosetta suite. This
paradigm produced landmark results, including de novo enzymes for reactions with
no natural catalyst \parencite{rothlisberger_kemp_2008,siegel_computational_2010}.
For assemblies specifically, symmetry was exploited explicitly: symmetric docking
principles \parencite{padilla_nanohedra_2001} were combined with atomic-level
interface design to produce self-assembling protein nanomaterials
\parencite{king_computational_2012}, co-assembling multi-component nanomaterials
\parencite{king_accurate_2014}, megadalton-scale two-component icosahedra
\parencite{bale_accurate_2016} and hyperstable 60-subunit cages
\parencite{hsia_design_2016}, supported by fast sequence-independent docking tools
for nanomaterial design \parencite{sheffler_fast_2023}. These methods work, but
they are laborious: they depend on hand-curated building blocks, expert-tuned
energy terms and extensive filtering.

\subsection{The machine-learning revolution in de novo design}
\label{subsec:ml-design}

The accuracy revolution in protein \emph{structure prediction} that includes AlphaFold2
\parencite{jumperHighlyAccurateProtein2021}, RoseTTAFold
\parencite{baekAccuratePredictionProtein2021} and, more recently, the all-atom
biomolecular model AlphaFold3 \parencite{abramsonAccurateStructurePrediction2024} transformed what is computationally tractable, and it was quickly followed by an
analogous revolution in \emph{generative} design. Deep generative models now
propose novel backbones directly: denoising-diffusion models such as RFdiffusion
\parencite{watsonNovoDesignProtein2023} and the programmable generative model
Chroma \parencite{ingrahamIlluminatingProteinSpace2023}, together with a rapidly
growing family of backbone and all-atom generators
\parencite{lin_generating_2023,lin_out_2024,linFastUltraCapableProtein2026,
geffner_proteina_2025}, and the recent all-atom RFdiffusion3
\parencite{butcher_novo_2025}. Sequences for these backbones are supplied by
learned inverse-folding models such as ProteinMPNN
\parencite{dauparasRobustDeepLearning2022}. The reach of this toolkit is
illustrated by de novo binders \parencite{cao_novo_2020,vazquez_torres_novo_2024},
atomically accurate designed antibodies \parencite{bennett_atomically_2026} and de
novo enzymes \parencite{yeh_novo_2023}; recent reviews chart the trajectory of the
field \parencite{yang_past_2026}.

The central premise of this report is that these two threads (a fully
protein-based vaccine platform, and a generative toolkit powerful enough to design proteins from scratch) are ready to be brought together. The next chapter makes
that case precise.
